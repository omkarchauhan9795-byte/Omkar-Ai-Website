The story

About Omkar-Ai

Built by one developer who believes your computer should understand you — not the other way around.

v3.0.0 CC BY-NC 4.0 Local-first

Not a product.
A personal infrastructure project.

Omkar-Ai started as a frustration: every "AI assistant" was either a cloud chatbot that forgot everything, or a local tool that couldn't actually do anything on the desktop. I wanted something that could hear me, see my screen, control my mouse and keyboard, remember our conversations, learn from its mistakes, and — critically — ask before it breaks things.

Three years, hundreds of commits, and 283 passing tests later, v3.0.0 delivers that vision: a dual-engine brain (Gemini Live + multi-provider router), Hermes Memory with a self-learning skill loop, Win32-level desktop hands guarded by a deterministic safety layer, background subagents for parallel work, and MCP extensibility — all running locally on Windows with no GPU required.

This is free for personal use, licensed CC BY-NC 4.0. No telemetry, no forced updates, no account required. Your API keys, memory, and skills never leave your machine unless you send them to a provider.

Design principles

What guides every decision

01

Local-first, cloud-optional

Your data, keys, memory, and skills live on your disk. Providers are pluggable; the assistant works even if you swap the brain.

02

Permission is a feature

Destructive actions hit a hard guard. The model cannot confirm its own requests — only you can. Every "yes" is a deliberate human act.

03

Undo > confirm

Checkpoints before every risky operation. One click or "undo" rolls it back. Safety isn't just prevention — it's recovery.

04

Memory that compounds

Hermes Memory + self-learning skills mean the assistant gets better the longer you use it. It remembers your preferences, your projects, your corrections.

05

Parallel, not sequential

Subagents delegate complex work to background workers. You keep talking; they research, scrape, code, and report back.

06

Extensible by design

MCP, plugins, and skills let you add capabilities without touching core code. The tool bus is open.
